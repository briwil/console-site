An app for the Ableton Move

CONSOLE.

An 8-track tape recorder and groovebox that runs on your Move.

  • Sequence eight drum and synth tracks.
  • Dozens of synths, drums, and inserts.
  • Parameter locks and step parameters.
  • Layout patterns in Song mode.
  • Record, splice, cut, and drop to eight tape tracks.
  • Export final bounces and grab via browser.
  • Mirror the screen to any web browser.

The tape

Eight tracks. Real splices.

Record on a track, then edit it the way you would on a tape machine.

Arm and record

Hold Shift and tap a step button to arm a track. Armed tracks glow red, and one press of Record records them all, so a drum and a synth can go down together.

Splice, copy, cut

Sample splits the box under the playhead. Hold Copy or Delete, turn the jog wheel to take more, and Capture drops it where the playhead is.

Loop and overdub

Set in and out points with the arrow keys, loop between them, and switch between replacing the tape and adding to it.

Gain and fades

Adjust gain, fade in and outs, and normalize easily from the Move's encoders.

Drum machines

The left 4 × 4 pads are the drums. The right 4 × 4 play the picked drum chromatically, a semitone a pad.

Synths

The pads play a scale, with the +/- buttons moving the octave. Knobs 1-8 follow the page you are on.

A sampler

16 samples, one per drum, each loaded from the Move's own sample folders, with level, pitch, start, decay and pan.

Sequencer

Sixteen steps, no limits on feel

Every instrument has its own sequencer. Every drum has its own sequencer. Every note can have its own sequencer.

Per-lane rate

Each drum runs at its own rate, with Euclidean fills.

Step settings

Hold a step to lock a different tune, pulse or length on just that hit.

Triplets

One menu setting turns the grid into triplets. LFOs and the arp have triplet rates too.

LFOs and arp

Two LFOs per pattern and an arpeggiator.

Effects

What you hear is what you record

Each instrument has a reverb send, a delay send and two insert effects. Two more global inserts can be added, and everything gets recorded onto tape. Tape tracks can be mono or stereo, inputs can be individual drums or the whole kit or synth.

Getting started

Five steps to Console

  1. Allow your computer to connect

    On your computer, open Terminal (Mac) or PowerShell (Windows). Make a key by running this and pressing Enter at each question. Skip it if you already have a key.

    ssh-keygen -t ed25519

    Then show the key. Copy the line it prints and paste it into the box at move.local/development/ssh, opened in your computer's browser on the same Wi-Fi as the Move.

    Maccat ~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub
    Windowstype $env:USERPROFILE\.ssh\id_ed25519.pub

  2. Install

    You need RNBO Move Takeover on your Move first. Then paste this into Terminal on your computer and wait a few minutes.

    ssh ableton@move.local "python3 -c 'import sys,urllib.request as u;exec(u.urlopen(sys.argv[1]).read())' https://raw.githubusercontent.com/briwil/console-move/main/move/install.py"

    Not live yet: this works once the project is public.

  3. Start Console

    Hold Shift and press Step 2 to open the Move's settings, then choose RNBO at the bottom of the list. Console opens by itself after a few seconds. The menu item keeps the name RNBO.

  4. Press Record

    Pick your instruments. Play, live record, or sequence.
    Switch to tape mode, arm your tracks and record your tracks.
    Splice, lift, drop- a final wav file is written after every edit.

  5. Bounce and quit

    Bounce your tracks, grab them at http://move.local and quit back to the original Move OS when you're done. No harm, no foul.
